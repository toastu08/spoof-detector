# E1.1 — Is the mel spectrogram necessary?

**Experiment:** E1.1 (ROADMAP phase 1). **Status: complete (2026-11-23).**
**Pre-registration:** [`PREREGISTRATION.md`](PREREGISTRATION.md) (written before any
probe was trained).

[`per_attack_eer.csv`](per_attack_eer.csv), [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md) (generated by
`scripts/summarize_e1_1.py`).
**Exploratory analysis:** `notebooks/results_analysis.ipynb`.
**Git commit at training time:** `9881dbf6a5cafe8fe566f035d32b86b00a8cba21`,
working tree **dirty** (see "Threats to validity").
**Data manifest:** `manifests/train_seed42.csv`, SHA-256
`22f98abdf3392e04f7df3632809f81d033d5687773946cd103f5066846ed195e`,
10,080 rows (2,580 bonafide / 7,500 spoof).

## Summary

Six time-frequency front-ends were rendered to `magma` images and each was given a
freshly trained classifier head on the **same frozen ImageNet ResNet18 backbone**,
with the same data, seed and training budget.

- **H1 — supported, with a refinement.** Mel resolution matters up to 128 bands;
  beyond 128 there is no measurable generalisation gain in this regime.
- **H2 — partially supported, mechanism refined.** A non-mel front-end
  (`linear_stft`) reaches baseline-like dev performance, so the mel warping is not
  required for in-domain discrimination. The mel front-end buys a modest
  advantage under unseen-attack shift, not a qualitative one.
- **H3 — supported as stated, interpretation withdrawn.** The random-projection
  control is clearly worse in aggregate, but the pre-registered "image-statistics
  shortcut" inference is **not testable in this design** and is withdrawn.

The most interesting result is not the ranking but the **per-attack reshuffling**:
the aggregate eval EER ordering hides per-attack swings of 20+ points, and
`linear_stft` and `randproj128` each win 4 of the 13 eval attacks despite sitting
2.6 and 5.6 points behind in aggregate.

## Provenance and reproduction

Each condition differs only in its 2-D representation. Everything else is fixed:
16 kHz, 4 s / 64,000 samples, `power_to_db(ref=np.max)` (except MFCC), min-max
normalisation to `[0,1]`, `magma` colormap, `origin='lower'` flip, resize to
224×224, ImageNet normalisation.

| Front-end | Version string | Representation | Rendered shape (rows × frames) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mel64` | `mel64-magma-4s-v1` | 64-band mel, dB | 64 × 126 |
| `mel128` | `mel128-magma-4s-v1` | 128-band mel, dB (**control**) | 128 × 126 |
| `mel256` | `mel256-magma-4s-v1` | 256-band mel, dB | 256 × 126 |
| `linear_stft` | `linear1025-magma-4s-v1` | linear-frequency power STFT, dB | 1,025 × 126 |
| `mfcc40` | `mfcc40-magma-4s-v1` | 40 MFCC coefficients, no deltas | 40 × 126 |
| `randproj128` | `randproj128-magma-4s-v1` | fixed Gaussian projection of the linear dB STFT | 128 × 126 |

Training recipe per condition (from `Models/Iteration1/e_1_1/<frontend>/config.json`):
`torchvision` ResNet18 (`weights="DEFAULT"`), all backbone parameters frozen but
**BatchNorm left in train mode** so running statistics update, head
`Linear(512,128) → ReLU → Dropout(0.5) → Linear(128,1)` deterministically
re-initialised with `torch.manual_seed(42)`, unweighted `BCEWithLogitsLoss`, Adam
`lr=1e-3`, batch size 32, at most 20 epochs, early stopping with patience 5 on
full-dev BCE loss, seed 42.

## Hypotheses and verdicts

### H1 — resolution: **supported, with a refinement**

> *Pre-registered:* EER improves from `mel64` to `mel128` to `mel256`, with
> diminishing returns.

Confirmed monotonically on both splits, but the two steps behave differently:

- **64 → 128 is a large, real gain:** dev −4.37 points (10.05% → 5.68%), eval
  −1.60 points (13.01% → 11.41%).
- **128 → 256 is a large dev gain but a tie on eval:** dev −1.32 points
  (5.68% → 4.36%), eval −0.10 points (11.41% → 11.31%), while AUC is marginally
  lower for `mel256` (0.9525 vs 0.9536). The clean estimate cannot separate them.

**Refinement to the pre-registered wording:** extra mel resolution buys *in-domain
fit*, not *unseen-attack generalisation*. The claim "performance is sensitive to
mel resolution" holds only up to 128 bands in this regime.

### H2 — modality: **partially supported, mechanism refined**

> *Pre-registered:* a non-mel representation (`linear_stft` or `mfcc40`) is within
> a small margin of `mel128` on eval, suggesting the backbone mostly uses generic
> spectral texture rather than the mel warping.

The two non-mel conditions give different answers, so the verdict is split:

- **`linear_stft` — competitive in aggregate.** It *ties* `mel128` on dev (5.69%
  vs 5.68%) and is 2.57 points worse on eval (13.98% vs 11.41%). Crucially that
  gap is a **trade, not a uniform loss**: it beats `mel128` on A07 (−0.39), A08
  (−1.21), A16 (−1.31) and, decisively, on **A19 (6.94% vs 18.82%, −11.88)**, while
  losing badly on A12 (+10.11) and A13 (+11.03).
- **`mfcc40` — clearly behind.** 18.56% eval EER (+7.15 points), lowest AUC
  (0.8963), and it wins **0 of 13** eval attacks. Its losses are largest on the
  hard families (A17 40.00%, A18 40.21%, A19 36.25% vs `mel128`'s
  34.39 / 18.56 / 18.82), but it is also behind on **every** easier attack
  (A07–A16: +1.5 to +8.2 points), and on dev it is already 14.49% vs 5.68%. It is
  worse on both fit and transfer, not only on the unseen attacks.

**Verdict:** the backbone reaches baseline-like dev performance **without** the mel
warping, so the mel front-end is not what makes the approach work in-domain. What
mel appears to buy is a modest robustness advantage under unseen-attack shift — and
even that is not clean, since `linear_stft` beats `mel128` by 11.9 points on A19.

**Therefore we do *not* claim "the model performs best on mel warping" as a
qualitative result.** The defensible statement is narrower: `mel128`/`mel256` are
best in aggregate among these six, and the margin over a non-mel condition is 2.57
eval points with mirrored per-attack wins on both sides.

### H3 — control: **supported as stated, interpretation withdrawn**

> *Pre-registered:* `randproj128` is clearly worse than `mel128`; if it is
> competitive, the result is suspicious of an image-statistics shortcut and must be
> investigated before any claim.

**As stated, supported.** `randproj128` is the second-worst condition:
+5.63 eval EER points, −0.047 AUC, worst `FPR@0.5` by a wide margin (55.46% vs
19.61%), and roughly **double** the EER on the two hardest families (A18 41.03% vs
18.56%, A19 36.91% vs 18.82%). This is not a competitive result.

**The pre-registered inference is withdrawn, for a structural reason.** Two facts
make "suspicious of an image-statistics shortcut" untestable in this design:

1. `randproj128` is a fixed Gaussian projection of the **linear dB STFT**, not
   random noise. It retains the full spectral content in a scrambled basis, so it
   is a *dimensionality/layout* control, not a shortcut control.
2. All six conditions share the same `magma` colormap, min-max normalisation and
   vertical flip. Any rendering shortcut would therefore be **shared by every
   condition and invisible to this experiment**.

What does survive is a weaker, valid inference: if the model only needed global
image statistics, scrambling the row order of spectral energy would not cost much.
It costs 5.63 points — so **the model does use time-frequency layout**, not just
global image texture. That supports the "generic spectral texture" mechanism in H2
while leaving the colormap question to E1.2 and the silence/noise/duration
shortcut controls to E1.4.

## Findings

**1. Aggregate EER hides large per-attack reshuffling — the headline result.**
The six conditions do not simply order themselves by quality; they rank differently
per attack, with signed per-attack swings of up to ~23 points (`linear_stft`) and
~26 points (`randproj128`) between their best and worst attacks:

- `randproj128` beats `mel128` on **A10 (3.56% vs 6.73%), A12 (4.05% vs 6.69%),
  A14 (4.21% vs 4.52%) and A15 (5.19% vs 6.12%)** — 4 of 13 attacks — while being
  roughly twice as bad on A18/A19.
- `linear_stft` wins 4 of 13, including **A19 by 11.88 points** (its worst loss
  is A13 at +11.03, a 22.9-point swing).
- `mel64` wins 5 of 13.

Two consequences: the pooled eval EER is largely driven by A17–A19, where all six
conditions fail; and a single aggregate number is a weak basis for model selection.
Any downstream comparison (E1.3, E3.4) should report per-attack metrics.

**2. Mel resolution saturates at 128 for generalisation but not for fit.** Dev EER
keeps improving to 256 bands (4.36% vs 5.68%) while eval EER is flat
(11.31% vs 11.41%) and AUC slightly favours 128. Extra frequency resolution is
absorbed by the fixed 224×224 input and does not transfer to unseen attacks.

**3. `mel256`'s threshold-0.5 advantage is a score-shift effect, not better
separation.** `mel256` has a much lower `FPR@0.5` than `mel128` on both splits
(dev 12.79% vs 22.37%, eval 11.53% vs 19.61%) with equal dev recall (98.78% vs
98.78%) and a 3.4-point eval recall cost (88.80% vs 92.32%). But its AUC is
marginally *lower*, and its dev-selected EER threshold is much closer to the
default (0.832 vs 0.916). So the difference at `0.5` is mostly **where each model's
score distribution sits**, not how well it ranks. This is exactly the uncalibrated
threshold artefact that E2.5 must address; it mirrors the weighted-vs-unweighted
result in E0.3, where weighting mainly shifted the operating point.

**4. The `linear_stft` tie is on dev, not eval.** `linear_stft` and `mel128` are
indistinguishable on the model-selection split (5.69% vs 5.68%) and separate only on
the clean estimate (13.98% vs 11.41%). The mel warping therefore contributes
shift-robustness rather than in-domain discriminability — a distinction that would
be invisible if only dev were reported.

**5. The weak conditions stop improving early and lose most on the hardest
families.** `mfcc40` and `randproj128` were the only conditions to trigger early
stopping (best epochs 10 and 11, stopped at 15 and 16); the other four ran the full
20-epoch budget, so the weak pair saturated dev loss sooner. Their aggregate penalty
is dominated by A18/A19 (+21.65 / +17.42 points for `mfcc40`, +22.47 / +18.09 for
`randproj128`), with smaller but still clear losses on several easier attacks
(`mfcc40`: +6.39 to +8.22 on A13, A14 and A16). That is consistent with a weaker
representation on both fit and transfer, but this report does not separate the two;
the per-attack table is the evidence, and `train_log.csv` holds the training losses
if that decomposition is needed.

**6. The 20-epoch budget is close to binding.** Four of six conditions ran to the
cap (best epochs 18, 20, 15, 19). `mel256` selected epoch 15 by dev loss but had a
slightly *better* in-loop dev EER at epoch 20 (4.29% vs 4.36%), so the loss-based
selection criterion and the EER we report are not perfectly aligned. This is a
criterion choice, not a bug, but it means dev EER is not each condition's best
achievable value.

## Threats to validity

1. **Single seed, no significance testing.** Every condition is one training run.
   A back-of-envelope binomial standard error on eval EER (7,355 bonafide,
   63,882 spoof, FPR≈FNR≈0.11) is roughly 0.2–0.4 points, so: the 0.10-point
   `mel256`/`mel128` gap is a **tie**; the 1.60- and 2.57-point gaps are beyond
   eval sampling error but **within unmeasured training-seed variance**; the 5.63-
   and 7.15-point gaps are robust. Treat the 1–3 point differences as "modestly
   behind", not "decisively worse". The pre-registered limitation stands: no
   confidence intervals or significance tests are implemented.
2. **The pre-registered "competitive" rule cannot discriminate.** It defines
   competitive as "small relative to the per-attack variation already present",
   but that spread is 1–36 points, which would admit almost any result. This is
   logged as a deviation: verdicts are given descriptively (aggregate delta plus
   per-attack trade-offs) rather than by mechanically applying the rule.
3. **The runs were made on a dirty working tree** at commit `9881dbf`, as recorded
   in every `config.json` (`"dirty": true`). Per G9 this is not reproducible from a
   commit alone. The front-end and training code was committed afterwards in
   `477ae71`, but that commit is not the exact tree that produced the checkpoints.
4. **BatchNorm statistics adapt per front-end.** The backbone *parameters* are
   frozen and shared, but train-mode BN updates running statistics from each
   condition's own images, so the effective backbone differs between conditions.
   Part of the measured difference could be BN adaptation quality rather than the
   representation itself. This is a documented consequence of the
   baseline-faithful recipe, not an accident.
5. **The 224×224 resize confounds "resolution".** `mel256` keeps 224/256 = 87.5% of
   its rows, `mel128` is upsampled 75%, `linear_stft` is downsampled to 224/1025 =
   21.9%, and `mfcc40` upsamples 40 → 224. "Resolution" here means *resolution as
   seen at the model input*, not raw spectral resolution.
6. **Head-only regime.** Conclusions are about a frozen ImageNet backbone plus a
   65,793-parameter head. They do not transfer to fine-tuned or waveform models.
7. **Dev is a model-selection split.** Early stopping used full dev, and dev EER is
   therefore optimistic and not comparable in level to eval EER. Dev→eval "gaps"
   conflate attack difficulty with generalisation and must not be read as
   generalisation error (they correlate inversely with dev performance: −2.67 points
   for `randproj128`, +8.29 for `linear_stft`).
8. **The E1.1 images are outside `make audit-data`'s current scope.** They are
   rendered from the audited `data/LA` with the audited audio contract, and
   `mel128` is pixel-identical to the audited export, but the 637k new PNGs have
   not themselves been hash-audited. Extending the audit is an open item.
9. **One corpus only.** Everything is ASVspoof 2019 LA; the A17–A19 difficulty is a
   property of this corpus's attack families.

## Decision and next steps

- **Keep `mel128` as the canonical preprocessing contract.** `mel256` is at best
  tied on the clean estimate (0.10 points), and G3 requires retraining and
  re-freezing the baseline for any front-end change. A 0.10-point gain does not
  justify invalidating every existing checkpoint and metric.
- **`mel256` is the candidate of record** if the front-end is ever changed for
  another reason: it is better on dev, better at the `0.5` threshold on both
  splits, and tied on eval. Its threshold behaviour should be re-examined under
  calibration (E2.5), not adopted on the strength of `FPR@0.5`.
- **Do not describe the mel front-end as necessary.** Use the H2 wording above.
- Recommended follow-up, cheapest first:
  1. **Multi-seed replication** of `mel128`, `mel256`, `linear_stft` and
     `randproj128` (3 extra seeds) to put error bars on the 1–3 point differences.
     Head-only, so a few GPU-hours. Note that train-mode BN prevents caching
     backbone features; `--freeze-bn` would allow caching but changes the recipe.
  2. **E1.2** for the colormap/orientation question E1.1 structurally cannot answer.
  3. **E1.4** for the silence/noise/duration shortcut controls.
  4. Extend `make audit-data` to cover `data/derived/e1_1_frontend/`.
