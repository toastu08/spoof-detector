## Summary

Eight rendering conditions over the **fixed 128-band mel representation** were each
given a freshly trained classifier head on the **same frozen ImageNet ResNet18
backbone**, with the same data, seed and training budget. Only how the dB matrix is
turned into a PNG changes.

- **H1 (colormap) — not supported.** Grayscale is fully competitive with `magma`
  and `viridis`. There is no consistent penalty for removing chroma; on the flipped
  condition, `gray_flip` even beats `viridis_flip`.
- **H2 (orientation) — suggestive interaction, not a shortcut.** Orientation moves
  EER by 0.5–1.7 points, but its **sign depends on the colormap**: `magma` is
  *better* flipped (the existing contract), while `gray` and `viridis` are better
  unflipped. No pair reaches the pre-registered 5-point "shortcut" bar.
- **H3 (contrast normalisation) — not supported; a clean null.** `rawdb_flip`
  (fixed `[-80, 0]` dB) is **identical** to `gray_flip` (per-clip min-max) to the
  4th decimal. The frozen backbone does not lean on per-clip contrast.
- **H4 (encoding) — confirmed.** `magma_flip_rgb` reproduces `magma_flip` exactly
  (ΔEER 0.00), so RGBA-vs-RGB encoding is irrelevant.

The operational verdict is unchanged: **the canonical `mel128` + `magma` +
`origin='lower'` contract is not beaten by more than noise**, so no preprocessing
change is warranted.

## Provenance and reproduction

Each condition differs only in its rendering. Everything else is fixed: 16 kHz,
4 s / 64,000 samples, 128-band mel, `power_to_db(ref=np.max)`, resize to 224×224,
ImageNet normalisation.

| Condition | Colormap | Orientation | dB scaling | Encoding | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `magma_flip` | magma | low frequency bottom (`lower`) | per-clip min-max | RGBA | **control** (existing contract) |
| `viridis_flip` | viridis | low frequency bottom | per-clip min-max | RGBA | rich-RGB alternative |
| `gray_flip` | gray | low frequency bottom | per-clip min-max | RGBA | no chroma |
| `rawdb_flip` | gray | low frequency bottom | fixed `[-80, 0]` dB | RGBA | contrast-normalisation probe |
| `magma_noflip` | magma | low frequency top (`upper`) | per-clip min-max | RGBA | orientation |
| `viridis_noflip` | viridis | low frequency top | per-clip min-max | RGBA | orientation × colormap |
| `gray_noflip` | gray | low frequency top | per-clip min-max | RGBA | orientation × colormap |
| `magma_flip_rgb` | magma | low frequency bottom | per-clip min-max | RGB | encoding control |

Training recipe per condition (from
`Models/Iteration1/e_1_2/<condition>/seed42/config.json`): `torchvision` ResNet18
(`weights="DEFAULT"`), all backbone parameters frozen but **BatchNorm left in train
mode** so running statistics update, head `Linear(512,128) → ReLU → Dropout(0.5) →
Linear(128,1)` deterministically re-initialised with `torch.manual_seed(42)`,
unweighted `BCEWithLogitsLoss`, Adam `lr=1e-3`, batch size 32, at most 20 epochs,
early stopping with patience 5 on full-dev BCE loss, seed 42.

the frozen baseline through the same code path. It does, exactly:

The control reproduces E1.1 `mel128` to zero decimal places, and the two

### The three single-variable comparisons

Three pairs differ in exactly one rendering degree of freedom, which isolates the
cleanest signals in the experiment:

| Variable changed | Pair | eval ΔEER | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| PNG encoding (RGBA→RGB) | `magma_flip` vs `magma_flip_rgb` | **0.00** | Encoding is irrelevant |
| dB scaling (min-max→fixed [-80,0]) | `gray_flip` vs `rawdb_flip` | **0.00** | Contrast normalisation is irrelevant |
| Orientation (flip→noflip) | magma / gray / viridis | **+0.52 / −1.09 / −1.69** | Small, colormap-dependent effect |

The first two are genuine nulls: EER identical to the 4th decimal and AUC differing
only at the 4th decimal (`rawdb_flip` 0.9418 vs `gray_flip` 0.9420). The third is
the one non-null signal, and its sign flips with the colormap.

## Hypotheses and verdicts

### H1 — colormap: **not supported**

> *Pre-registered:* `magma_flip` and `viridis_flip` are close and both better than
> `gray_flip`; grayscale losing materially would support the ImageNet-RGB-statistics
> reading.

Grayscale does **not** lose. On the flipped condition the ranking is `magma`
11.41% < `gray` 12.44% < `viridis` 12.94% — gray *beats* viridis. On the unflipped
condition it is `viridis` 11.26% < `gray` 11.35% < `magma` 11.92% — gray beats
magma. So chroma buys nothing consistent, and "more colour = better" is directly
contradicted by `viridis`, the most saturated map, being the worst flipped condition.

The pre-registered "colormap cosmetic" rule (three flipped conditions within
1.0 point) is *not* met — the flipped spread is 1.53 points — but the "colormap
difference claimed" rule (≥5 points) is nowhere near met. The honest verdict is
that the colormap differences are **suggestive (1–3 points), not decisive**, and
the direction runs against the chroma-richness hypothesis.

### H2 — orientation: **suggestive interaction, not a shortcut**

> *Pre-registered:* each no-flip condition is close to its flipped counterpart; a
> large gap would identify orientation as a shortcut.

Orientation is the one variable that consistently moves EER, but the sign depends
on the colormap:

| Pair | eval Δ (noflip − flip) | dev Δ (noflip − flip) | Better orientation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `magma_flip` → `magma_noflip` | **+0.52** | +0.32 | **flip** (existing contract) |
| `gray_flip` → `gray_noflip` | −1.09 | −0.43 | noflip |
| `viridis_flip` → `viridis_noflip` | −1.69 | −1.30 | noflip |

By the pre-registered rules this is **not a shortcut**: no pair reaches the 5-point
bar, and only `viridis` (1.69) exceeds the 1.5-point "close" threshold while
`magma` (0.52) and `gray` (1.09) stay inside it. The result is an **interaction** —
orientation matters a little, and the best orientation is colormap-specific — not
a uniform "no-flip wins". For `magma`, the canonical flipped orientation is already
the better of the two, so the original author's choice was correct.

### H3 — contrast normalisation: **not supported (null result)**

> *Pre-registered:* `rawdb_flip` differs from `gray_flip`; a material `rawdb_flip`
> advantage would indicate that removing per-clip contrast normalisation preserves
> spoofing/channel information.

They do **not** differ: eval EER 12.44% = 12.44%, dev EER 5.53% = 5.53%, AUC
0.9418 vs 0.9420. The pre-registered "3 points better" threshold is met with a
delta of **0.00**. The informative reading is the null: the frozen backbone is
**indifferent to per-clip contrast stretch**, so contrast normalisation is not a
shortcut it exploits. (This is a single-variable comparison — both conditions are
grayscale, flipped, RGBA — so it is the cleanest contrast probe in the experiment.)

### H4 — encoding: **confirmed**

> *Pre-registered:* `magma_flip_rgb` is pixel-identical to `magma_flip` and
> therefore differs by approximately zero; any material difference is a pipeline
> bug.

Confirmed exactly: ΔEER **0.00**, identical AUC/FPR/recall on both splits. RGBA
versus RGB encoding is irrelevant; the check also acts as a bug-catcher and passed.

## Findings

**1. No change to the canonical rendering.** `magma_flip` (the existing contract)
sits essentially at the frontier: the best alternative, `viridis_noflip`, beats it
by only **0.15 points** on eval (11.26% vs 11.41%), which is within noise. Nothing
in this experiment justifies invalidating the AGENTS.md §4.2 contract.

**2. Contrast normalisation is doing no work.** `gray_flip` and `rawdb_flip`
(identical grayscale images except per-clip min-max vs fixed `[-80, 0]` dB) train
to the same behaviour. Whatever the model uses, it is invariant to this contrast
stretch. This directly answers part of the "shortcut" question E1.1 could not reach:
a contrast-based rendering shortcut is ruled out.

**3. Chroma does not help; luminance is the more plausible channel.** The colormap
RGB-locus table (below) shows the three maps differ in both chroma and luminance,
so the effect cannot be attributed to one alone — but the fact that grayscale (zero
chroma) is competitive at every orientation rules out a strong chroma benefit.
Notably `viridis`, the most saturated map (0.458) and the one with the *smallest*
luminance range (0.786), is the worst flipped condition, while `gray` has full
luminance range (1.000) and the highest per-channel standard deviation (0.290).
This is consistent with a luminance-not-chroma reading, but is **not proven** — it
is an interpretation grounded in measured locus statistics, not a test.

| Colormap | R range | G range | B range | Mean channel sd | Mean saturation | Luminance range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gray | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.290 | 0.000 | 1.000 |
| magma | 0.996 | 0.991 | 0.736 | 0.261 | 0.414 | 0.971 |
| viridis | 0.874 | 0.901 | 0.463 | 0.214 | 0.458 | 0.786 |

**4. The colormap ranking inverts with orientation.** Among flipped conditions
`magma < gray < viridis`; among unflipped conditions `viridis < gray < magma`. There
is no colormap main effect — only an interaction with orientation. This is why the
pooled "which colormap is best" question is ill-posed without fixing the orientation.

**5. The `FPR@0.5` spread is a score-shift artefact, not better separation.** The
control has the *highest* `FPR@0.5` (19.61%) and *highest* spoof recall@0.5 (92.32%)
of all eight conditions, while its threshold-free AUC is the best; the no-flip
conditions have lower `FPR@0.5` but also lower recall. As in E1.1, the difference at
`0.5` reflects where each model's score distribution sits (their dev-selected
thresholds range 0.825–0.916), not how well they rank. This is the uncalibrated
threshold artefact that E2.5 must address.

**6. The orientation effect is concentrated in a few attacks.** The largest flip
swings are on the hard families: `gray` on A17 (39.82% → 35.31%, −4.5 pts) and
`viridis` on A19 (18.19% → 13.31%, −4.9 pts) and A13 (12.55% → 10.05%, −2.5 pts).
The pooled eval EER is dominated by A17–A19, where every condition fails; the
aggregate orientation gap is driven by a handful of attacks, consistent with E1.1's
finding that per-attack reshuffling is the largest effect.

## Threats to validity

1. **Single seed, no significance testing.** Every condition is one training run.
   A back-of-envelope binomial standard error on eval EER (7,355 bonafide,
   63,882 spoof, FPR≈FNR≈0.11) is roughly 0.2 points, so the 0.15-point
   `viridis_noflip`/`magma_flip` gap is a **tie**, and the 0.5–1.7 point gaps are
   beyond eval sampling error but **within unmeasured training-seed variance**. Only
   the 0.00 nulls (H3, H4) are robust by construction. The pre-registered
   limitation stands: no confidence intervals or significance tests are implemented.
2. **The runs were made on a dirty working tree** at commit `4fb53d685`, as recorded
   in each `config.json` (`"dirty": true`; the export ran clean). Per G9 this is not
   reproducible from a commit alone: the exact tree that produced the checkpoints
   contains untracked E1.2 artifacts.
3. **BatchNorm statistics adapt per condition.** Backbone *parameters* are frozen and
   shared, but train-mode BN updates running statistics from each condition's own
   images, so the effective backbone differs between conditions. Part of a measured
   difference could be BN adaptation quality rather than the rendering itself — a
   documented consequence of the baseline-faithful recipe.
4. **The 224×224 resize + ImageNet normalisation confound "grayscale".** A gray PNG
   becomes slightly non-gray after per-channel mean/std normalisation (the three
   ImageNet channels have different means/standard deviations), so "grayscale" is
   grayscale *before* the inference transform, not after. This is held fixed across
   conditions and was pre-registered.
5. **Colormap comparisons are not single-variable.** `magma`/`viridis`/`gray` differ
   in both chroma *and* luminance; only the encoding, contrast and orientation pairs
   are clean single-variable comparisons.
6. **Head-only regime.** Conclusions are about a frozen ImageNet backbone plus a
   65,793-parameter head. They do not transfer to fine-tuned or waveform models.
7. **Dev is a model-selection split.** Early stopping used full dev, and dev EER is
   therefore optimistic. Dev→eval differences conflate attack difficulty with
   generalisation and must not be read as generalisation error (e.g. `viridis_noflip`
   shows a −1.17 dev delta but only −0.15 on eval).
8. **The E1.2 images are outside `make audit-data`'s current scope.** They are
   rendered from the audited `data/LA` with the audited audio contract, and
   `magma_flip` is pixel-identical to the audited export, but the ~637k new PNGs
   have not themselves been hash-audited. Extending the audit is an open item.
9. **One corpus only.** Everything is ASVspoof 2019 LA; the A17–A19 difficulty is a
   property of this corpus's attack families.

## Decision and next steps

- **Keep `mel128` + `magma` + `origin='lower'` as the canonical preprocessing
  contract.** No condition beats it by more than 0.15 points on the clean estimate,
  and G3 requires retraining and re-freezing the baseline for any rendering change.
- **Contrast normalisation is confirmed unnecessary** for the frozen backbone, but
  it is also harmless, and it is part of the existing contract — do not remove it
  without a reason that outranks the re-freeze cost.
- **Do not describe `magma` as necessary, or grayscale as inferior.** Use the H1
  wording above: chroma buys nothing in this regime.
- Recommended follow-up, cheapest first:
  1. **Multi-seed replication** of `magma_flip`, `viridis_flip`, `viridis_noflip`
     and `gray_noflip` (3 extra seeds) to put error bars on the orientation
     interaction and confirm the 0.5–1.7 point swings are not seed artefacts. The
     makefile already accepts `E1_2_SEEDS`. Head-only, so a few GPU-hours; note that
     train-mode BN prevents caching backbone features.
  2. **E1.4** for the silence/noise/duration shortcut controls, which this
     experiment does not touch.
  3. Extend `make audit-data` to cover `data/derived/e1_2_rendering/`.

## Deviations from pre-registration

None. The experiment ran exactly as registered: all eight conditions, seed 42,
fixed recipe, and the decision rules in `PREREGISTRATION.md` were applied as
written. The verdicts above follow those rules directly — the rules produced
"non-decisive" outcomes for H1 and H2 (by design, given the single-seed
limitation) rather than clean binary confirm/refute results, which is not a
deviation.
